We train agents to fight in an unmodified commercial 3D game, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, under the Dolphin emulator. The agent never sees a pixel: it reads the emulator's memory and receives every actor's position, velocity, health and decoded animation routine. This removes the pixel-processing problem, not the perception problem, since nothing in the 1033-number observation is labelled.

A three-enemy melee encounter is solved by recurrent PPO in under five hours on twenty CPU machines. The dungeon boss Queen Gohma is not, and why is the useful content here. She takes damage only while stunned, a stun must be manufactured, and from full health a kill is ten sword hits across roughly five such openings. Our best policy defeats her on 8.33% of 456 matched evaluation episodes; the per-start profile ranges from 28.95% to 0 of 76, ordered by what each start hands the policy rather than by any single variable.

Four alternatives were measured against that policy under matched conditions and all four lost. Behaviour cloning on 100 recorded human victories reaches 74.5% held-out next-action accuracy and takes 0.33 of her 10 health, winning 1 of 80. Fifteen mechanisms for folding those demonstrations into the policy gradient produced no arm trending toward the policy trained without them. Offline return-conditioned cloning reaches 1.31 health. Policies trained inside learned world models won 0 of 607 real episodes.

Half the report concerns measurement, the larger source of error here than any method. A defeat is a 1 to 10% binary event; read through it, six consecutive versions appeared flat while the graded quantity the reward pays for rose 8.7-fold, and the two experiments launched to break that apparent plateau were themselves measurable regressions. We report a per-start profile rather than a pooled rate, and list eleven claims this project made internally and then withdrew.
